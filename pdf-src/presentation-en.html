What it is

A Telegram bot that searches for jobs in Denmark, compares them with your CV using AI, and drafts ready-to-use application documents — so you don't have to do it by hand every time.

How it searches for jobs

The bot fetches jobs directly from Jobindex.dk and Jobnet.dk (Denmark's official public job portal) based on your search terms, with an option to filter by city. Results from several sources are merged, and duplicates are removed automatically.

How it compares with your CV

It isn't just a search for matching words. The bot sends both your CV and each job posting to an AI model (Google Gemini) and asks it to assess the substance, not just formal overlap:

  • Whether your experience really matches the core tasks of the role
  • Whether there is a genuine professional overlap, or only a superficial technical similarity
  • What specifically matches, and what is missing

The result is a match percentage (0–100%) with a short explanation. If the CV has no relevant experience for the role, the bot honestly shows 0% — no "consolation percentages". The job still stays on the list — whether to apply is always the person's own choice.

Getting started

No setup beyond what the bot asks for itself: search terms, an optional city and a CV (PDF or Word). The bot guides you step by step with buttons, so you don't need to remember any commands.

Typos in city names are corrected automatically

No AI guesswork here — a reliable lookup against the official list of Danish municipalities. A small typo ("Arhus") is quietly corrected to "Aarhus".

Honest match assessment in practice

The same CV (an experienced bookkeeper), tested against three different positions — to show that the assessment really changes with the substance and doesn't just repeat a fixed number.

Agentic search: the AI thinks for itself

Not a fixed list of synonyms programmed in advance. When a search returns 0 results, Google Gemini gets access to a real search tool (function calling) and decides for itself whether — and which — broader or synonymous word to try instead.

How it works

  • 0 results — the search term doesn't appear directly in any job posting, and the AI is brought in automatically instead of just showing "nothing found".
  • The AI suggests an alternative itself — at most 2 attempts with a real, new search term, and it looks at the actual result before possibly trying again.
  • Honest refusal of nonsense — if the search term makes no sense, the AI says so honestly instead of guessing a random word.

Real example — search term with a typo: "boghlder"

The word 'boghlder' is a typical misspelling of the Danish job title bogholder (bookkeeper), so I'll try searching with the correct spelling instead.
→ Tried «bogholder»: 61 jobs found.

Real example — meaningless search term: "abrakadabra"

"abrakadabra" is a magic word and meaningless text in a job context, and so it cannot be interpreted as a real field or a job title.

This feature was tested with real data during development, and a real weakness was found and fixed: at first, the AI guessed a plausible but wrong word ("pædagog" — teaching assistant) for the meaningless search term "abrakadabra", instead of rejecting it. The solution was to give the AI explicit permission to refuse unreasonable input.

From search result to finished application

The whole flow in practice, with two different jobs from the same list — the bot writes a new, personal application every time, not a template with filled-in fields.

What it generates for each job

When you pick a specific job, the bot drafts 2 documents:

  • An application in Danish — based on real facts from the CV, with no invented experience. If something is missing, it is mentioned honestly but tactfully, as a willingness to learn it quickly.
  • The job posting in Danish as a PDF, with the contact details extracted (name, phone, email).

Check and fix the application: Verify

Afterwards, the application can be checked and corrected with two buttons:

  • Verify (check for invented facts) — a new, independent AI review compares the application with the real CV and flags every claim that isn't in the CV. At the same time, it checks whether the application completely misses an important requirement from the job posting, either as experience or as an honestly mentioned gap.
  • Fix based on Verify — if Verify has found something, the bot corrects the application in a targeted way, only in the flagged places, and recommends running Verify again on the corrected version.

An honestly mentioned gap ("no experience with X yet, but a fast learner") is not an error. Verify only flags claims with no support in the CV.

The bot never sends anything by itself

The bot only drafts — it never sends an application on its own. The final step (review, adjust, send) is always the person's own.
